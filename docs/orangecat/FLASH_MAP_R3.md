# Orange Cat flash map for Release 3

**Version:** 0.2 (November 1, 2026; 0.1 reviewed by an independent agent, seventeen findings, folded in: section 8; design only, no code; for the owner)
**Tracks:** BUILD.md 7.22, FX_MODULES_PLAN.md 0.3, MODULE_FORMAT_R3.md 0.2, PROJECT_FORMAT_R3.md 0.6, storage.c "THE FLASH MAP", oc_module.c, oc_store.c, smp_slot.c, loader/ldr_core.c, stub/stub_core.h, docs/BOOT-SAFETY.md

## 1. The problem

The module area is 12 sectors (0xC8000..0xD3FFF). WHEEL takes 4 and SHAPES 6, so 2 are left, and no effect module fits: DUB TAPE's kernel and its tables are about 8,500 B (3 sectors; the host stays in the image as the generic host), the reverb modes about 3,050 B (1), the FX layer about 4,000 B (1). The Prophet (8 sectors) fits only instead of SHAPES. Release 3 takes SHAPES out of the firmware (the owner's decision: it ships as its module), which frees room in the image, not in the module area: the two are not next to each other, and the image sits under the boot stub and the update loader's rules. This document says where the module area's growth comes from, what it costs, and which rules it keeps.

**The update loader's rules** (loader/ldr_core.c, 0.10.1): it writes only the app area [0x4000, 0x93000), every sector of it, from a package whose flash.bin is exactly that long (`fl_size != LDR_APP_HI` is refused), skipping sectors already equal; the marker's sector first erased and last written; and it erases any data sector in [0x93000, 0xFC000) whose last 256 bytes look like one of its update records (the record rule: the pack and every module keep their sector tails clean, `pk_record_like`, three places). **The stub's rules** (stub_core.h): the marker at 0x91FE0 names the body's length and CRC; the body is capped below the marker's sector. **The cipher**: the app is read through the SFC decryption; `fl_plain_window_init` (hal/fm1_flash.h) opens the plaintext XIP window from 0x93000 up at boot, so a module runs in place only above it.

**The numbers of the build set today** (b1997d1, CI's switches): image 562,144 B = stub 3,808 + body 558,336; free in the body 15,104 B. With `MELODEE_SHAPES=0` the image is 538,616 B (measured on be253e3: 562,144 against 538,616, **23,528 B freed**; the old figure on an older image was 23,236): body 534,808, free 38,632 B (9 sectors and 1,768 B). RAM does not move (.data+.bss 94,552 both ways; SHAPES' 2,580 B of hot tables are taken from the arena on demand either way).

A module's sectors include its 256-byte header page, so the FX layer's 4,000 B take 2 sectors. SHAPES + WHEEL + DUB + the reverb modes + the FX layer = **16 sectors**, exactly (13 with WHEEL at its real size of 1 sector, section 6); the three engines alone (SHAPES, WHEEL, the Prophet) = 18; engines and effects together = 24 (21 with WHEEL at 1). **12 is not enough; 16 holds the effects set with nothing to spare; 18 holds the effects with 2 spare (5 with WHEEL at 1), or the three engines; never everything at once.** The Prophet stays an alternative to the effects, as it is to SHAPES today, unless WHEEL is re-cut to one sector and the owner gives up the spare.

## 4. Where the room can come from (the three honest sources)

**(A) The app area's tail: give the image's freed bytes to a second module region.** This is the owner's idea read literally. The body would be capped at 0x8D000 (136 sectors, 557,056 B) and 0x8D000..0x90FFF (4 sectors, 16 KB) would hold modules; with SHAPES out the body has 22,248 B free there (today's SHAPES-in body does not fit: the cap can ship only with SHAPES out). What it takes: (1) the loader writes only the body the package names and leaves 0x8D000..0x90FFF alone (a loader rule; the loader ships inside every package and runs from the package being installed, so the new rule applies from the first package that carries it, but **an older package installed later rewrites the whole area and erases the region's modules**, and the installer page re-installs them, as it does today); (2) the plaintext window's low end moves to 0x8D000 at boot (one register; the body ends under it); (3) the stub's CRC already covers only the body length, so the stub needs no change; (4) the start table gains the region's starts; (5) tools/fm1pkg_make.py's `BODY_MAX` and the loader's own length check both cap the body at 0x88000 (the stub's cap at the marker and the loader's `len <= 0x8C000` would not catch a body running into the region); (6) the plaintext window has one low bound, so at 0x8D000 the marker's sector (0x91000, written encrypted) and 0x92000 also read as plaintext through XIP: nothing reads them that way, said; (7) an older firmware cannot re-install a region module (it has no such starts), so "the page re-installs them" holds only for the new firmware; (8) the official loader and the recovery tool wipe the region as well. What it gives: 4 sectors, and only 4: the body's reserve (UI_BUILD.md: 8 KB) and the format revision's own cost (PROJECT_FORMAT_R3.md 11.2b: with SHAPES out 11,884 to 14,884 B above the reserve at the central ends, 3,384 at the worst) leave nothing for a fifth. The cost: a change to the one program whose fault bricks every unit's update, for 16 KB.

**(B) The sample slot's idle sectors: extend the module area downward.** The record store's sectors 18..39 (0xB2000..0xC7FFF, 22 sectors, 88 KB) are the internal sample slot's room: when the slot is *claimed* (smp_slot.c, at a boot that finds room for the records elsewhere) records are moved out of them and the store never hands them out again; sampling is Phase 9 (not started), and the store dump of October 26 shows them erased or holding old slot data. Giving the module area the top 6 of them makes it **18 sectors, 0xC2000..0xD3FFF, contiguous**; the sample slot keeps 16 sectors (64 KB, 0xB2000..0xC1FFF).

*What it takes* (the review's list; 0.1 named a third of it): storage.c `ST_MODULE_LO`/`ST_MODULE_SECTORS` and hal/fm1_flash.h `FL_MODULE_LO`; oc_module.c `OC_MOD_AREA`, the start table and the geometry table (section 6), the `.noinit` guard record (unclean[3], an 8-start notice byte: a new magic); editor.c's `MOD_INFO` reply, which sends a **literal 12** today and gains the starts' sectors (appended: compatible); the record store: `OCS_NSECT` 56 to 50, `ocs_addr`'s split (0..33 at 0xA0000, 34..49 at 0xEA000), `ocs_hdr[]`, `smp_resv_mask`, the assert `S0 + NSECT == 40`; smp_slot.c: `SMP_SLOT_NSECT` 16 and **`SMP_SLOT_MAX` down from 81,408 to at most 60,896 B** (16 x 3,840 - the headers; the `_Static_assert` on the room and two tests assert the old capacity; a sample longer than the new maximum already in a slot reads EMPTY after the upgrade: there is none, samples are disabled); the legacy bank objects `OBJ_BANK0..+3` (0xA0000..0xC7FFF, copy B of slot 3 at 0xC3000..0xC7FFF, inside the new area; no runtime writer, the backup inventory still reads their headers): **retired** (writes refused, reads "no record") with a `_Static_assert` that no `st_sector` lands in the module area, and storage_test.c adjusted; tools/ocflash.py's `area_lo`, tools/build.py's module link base, tests/module_build_test.py's counts; the web page (web/fm1oc.js: `OCM_SECTOR` [0, 6, 4], `OCM_LINK0` 0x020C4000 = XIP(0xC8000), the 8-start cap at the package check) and firmware/src/card_install.c's start table.

*The one-way door 0.1 missed:* a module package's `start` field is a sector index from `FL_MODULE_LO`, each start's relocated image is proven by a CRC the build computed for that start, and the page and the card keep an image only with that CRC. **Moving the base to 0xC2000 changes what every published .ocm means** (SHAPES, WHEEL, the Prophet; the SEM, HADES and op1r measurement modules): they would be refused ("damaged") until rebuilt. The way round it, chosen here: **the base stays 0xC8000 and the start table grows downward with new start numbers** (start 3 = sector -6, 4 = -4, 5 = -2, or every sector: section 6; the area is one contiguous range 0xC2000..0xD3FFF but the numbering keeps 0 at 0xC8000, so the three published packages, their starts and CRCs stay valid and only new packages carry the new starts). `MOD_INFO` reports each start's sector; the page's table and the card's follow. No published package is invalidated; a new effect module's package proves its starts as today.

*What it keeps:* the loader's record rule applies as to the area now (the planner keeps every module's sector tails clean); the loader never writes there; the stub, the marker, the cipher window and the pack are untouched; a module install still writes the header last and a replacement beside the old one; the record store's power-cut rule is untouched.

*The upgrade (the review's finding 3, a real hazard):* the claim is not guaranteed to have happened. A store with no room elsewhere leaves the slot **unclaimed** and keeps writing records in all 56 sectors, 34..39 included; declaring them outside the store would then cut a live record's current copy (the older copy wins, or the record is gone), and a module install would erase it. So the first boot of the new firmware **migrates**: it scans all 56 sectors as before, re-saves any live part found in 34..39 into the rest (smp_claim's own code over 6 sectors), and only then narrows the store; when there is no room to move a part, the six sectors stay the store's and the planner refuses installs into them (the area reads as 12 sectors until a later boot finds room). The planner never erases a sector that holds a live record part (the store's map is consulted). A stale part in 34..39 after the move is never read again (outside the scan), so no duplicate-sequence case arises.

*The downgrade (restated):* an older firmware's store does not reuse the sectors (its slot claim finds no store header in module bytes, claims the slot and reserves 18..39; the store never hands them out). The writers that can hurt a module at 0xC2000..0xC7FFF on an older firmware are the **slot's own** (editor commands 111..114: a sample write longer than 16 sectors, ED_SLOT_ERASE erasing all 22; samples are disabled, so only a test or a deliberate command) and its **installer**, which does not know the new starts and may place a module over one (within 0xC8000..0xD3FFF too, for any new start the old grid lacks). A module so overwritten fails its header CRC at the next upgrade and the installer page offers it again. The installer page's line (RELEASE3_PLAN.md section 4) says so.

**(C) Move something else out of the way.** The library group (23 sectors) or OTA staging could move, as the library moved in 0.9.1 with a migration at boot; the record store's area 2 (16 sectors at 0xEA000) could be re-cut. Each is a data migration with a power-cut story of its own, for sectors the module area cannot reach contiguously (the library's are not adjacent to it; area 2 is at 0xEA000). Not recommended for Release 3.

## 6. The start table and the geometry table (oc_module.c)

Today: three starts (sector 0, 6, 4 of the area), a geometry per engine (SHAPES 6 sectors at starts 0 or 6; the Prophet 8 at 0 or 4; WHEEL 4 at 0 or 6; the OC_CANDIDATES rows SEM, HADES, FMBELL, SUPERSAW, DDDD at WHEEL's geometry), and the page's own copy of the table. A package carries one relocatable image and, per start it names, the CRC of the relocated image (format 2); the page and the card keep an image only with that CRC, and the page refuses a package naming more than 8 starts. So "any start" is not free: each start a module may take must be proven by its build, and the cap of 8 must be lifted or respected.

For Release 3: **a start at every sector** of the 18 (FX_MODULES_PLAN.md section 11 step 0 says the same; an even grid would waste a sector on every odd-sized module: DUB 3, the reverb modes 1), numbered so that the published packages keep their meaning: starts 0, 1, 2 stay sectors 0, 6, 4 of 0xC8000; starts 3.. name the other sectors of 0xC8000..0xD3FFF and then the six below 0xC8000. A module's size in sectors comes from its header; the geometry table keeps only the per-engine cap (SHAPES 6, the Prophet 8, WHEEL 1: its 2,928 B fit one sector; the 4 of today were the grid's) and gains the effects' caps from their headers. The page's cap on starts rises to 18, and a package's size grows by about 20 B a start (the CRC and the relocation count), or the per-start proof is replaced by the inner header's own CRC: the owner's call at step 0. The planner's rule is the same (the first start whose span is free; a replacement written to a free span, the old one erased at the next boot). One-way doors: none for the published packages (their starts and CRCs are unchanged); the new starts are a one-way door for a *downgrade* only (section 4B).

## 10. Option A as built: the tail region (Round 22; design 0.3, November 10, 2026; the owner approved A)

### 10.1 What it is

### 10.3 The loader writes only the body, never the tail

`firmware/loader/ldr_core.c` (it ships inside every package and runs from RAM from the package being installed, so it is the one rule that matters):

1. the marker's `len` limit `<= 0x8C000` becomes `<= 0x89000` (LDR_BODY_MAX), so a body that would run into the tail is refused before any erase (-16 is new);
2. the sector loop writes **0x4000 (the stub, only if different), 0x5000 .. ceil((0x5000 + len)/0x1000)** (the body, never past 0x8E000), **0x92000** (cfg_tool, as today), and the marker's sector last. It **never writes or erases 0x8E000..0x90FFF**, and does not write the padding sectors between the body's end and 0x8E000 (they are 0xFF in the package and stale, harmless bytes on the unit: the stub's CRC covers `len` only);
3. a package **without a valid marker** (the legacy order wrote the whole area, tail included) is **refused** (-17) by this loader, before any erase. The safer reading: no unit loses a module to a package type nobody builds any more. (tests/ldr_test.c's legacy cases are changed to expect the refusal.)
4. the `fl_size != LDR_APP_HI` check and the package layout are unchanged: **a package is the same file**; the old loader, installing the same package, still writes the whole area.

The stub (stub_core.h `STUB_BODY_MAX` 0x8C000) is **not changed**: it is the one unprotected window's program and the loader rewrites it only when it differs; a stub that accepts a longer body than the loader will install is harmless, and a body over 0x89000 cannot be built. The marker format and the compensation bytes are unchanged.

### 10.4 The firmware: the window, the store rule, the start table

- **The plaintext window** (`hal/fm1_flash.h` `SFCENC_UNENC_L`, at both sites, and `fl_inval`'s range): from FL_XIP(0x93000) to **FL_XIP(0x8E000)** (`FL_TAIL_LO`). The body ends under it; the tail's modules read as plain through XIP, as the pack and the area do. One bound only: 0x91000 (the marker, written encrypted) and 0x92000 (cfg_tool) read as plaintext through XIP too; nothing reads them that way (said; checked by grep in the review). A 4 KB-aligned value: the register takes the same form as today's.
- **The store's rule** (`FL_STORE_OK`): FL_TAIL_LO 0x8E000 .. FL_TAIL_HI 0x91000 added, so the installer's erase and program reach exactly these three sectors and not the marker's (nor 0x8D000, the body's last sector).
- **The start table** (`oc_module.c`): 21 starts. `oc_mod_sect[18..20] = -58, -57, -56` (signed sectors from 0xC8000: 0x8E000 is 58 below). Every constraint that said "start sector + sectors <= OC_MOD_AREA" becomes "the span is inside the start's place" (`oc_mod_place(i)`: 0 area, 1 tail). `OC_MOD_STARTS` 21 widens: the `.noinit` guard record's per-start bytes (a new magic, "MOD6"; a record of another size is discarded, as option B's was, and so is the four sector tail's "MOD5"), MOD_INFO's reply, the notice bytes, the loops; the page's start table `OC_SECT` and `OC_STARTS`.
- **The geometry table**: WHEEL **one sector, every start** (its 2,928 B; the 4 were the grid's), SHAPES 6 sectors, the Prophet 8 (area only: the tail holds 3), an effect module its header's sectors (area or tail); the models module (kind 4): the area's 18 or the tail's 3, whichever its pick fits (the master carries a variant for every start the pick fits at, `places` in its manifest). A published WHEEL package (4 sectors, starts 0 and 6) still installs at its starts: its image is the same; its reach is now 1 sector. The re-cut WHEEL package carries a variant per start it may take.
- **MOD_INFO** gains, appended (compatible), the tail's sector count (0 on a firmware without the region): the page and the card read it, and a unit without it has the area alone. The page treats `tail` as the master's `places` do.
- **Foreign bytes**: on a unit upgraded from a build whose body ran past 0x8E000 the region holds old body bytes (encrypted app code). A start whose header magic is not a module is EMPTY for the planner and **erased before any write** as every install does (`oc_mod_erase_span`); nothing is read as a module without its magic and CRC.

### 10.5 The planner, the meter and the page

The loader's planner (`oc_mod_plan`, `oc_fxmod_plan`, the models id's span) is extended to places; `web/fm1place.js` ports it again and `web/test_place.mjs` runs it against `tests/plan_oracle.c` (the C functions themselves) over many unit states including tail starts, one place full, the low six off, the page's own install order. The room meter (`PICK.room` with the places; the page's `modelsPlaces`) counts both regions and, with the tail on the unit, the placement is the planner's, not a sum. The default pick (the 14 models, 9 sectors) goes in the area; the models module does not go in the tail unless its pick is 3 sectors or fewer; WHEEL (1), DUB (3) and the reverb mode (1) go to the tail first when they fit: the planner's order is the page's install order (engine modules, effect modules, models last; on a unit with the tail the page's order is now the goal's, see "Built: the page's half of the rule" below) and each module takes the first allowed free start of the lowest place that holds it, so the area keeps its long runs for the pick. (The rule is the planner's, written once in the C and ported.)

**Found while porting the planner (2026-11-10): room is not enough, the goal needs a placement rule.** SHAPES may start at sector 0 or 6 only, and the planner gives it start 0 (every unit today): the area is then two runs of six (-6..-1 and 6..11), and **the 9-sector default pick has no run, with or without the tail** (`web/test_place.mjs`: "SHAPES alone then a 9 sector pick: refused"). The set of the goal fits exactly one way: SHAPES at start 1 (sectors 6..11), the models at -6..2 (9), WHEEL re-cut (1) and the FX layer (2) in 3..5, DUB TAPE (3) and the reverb modes (1) in the tail: 22 of 22. To get there: (a) the planner prefers, for SHAPES, the start that leaves the longer run (start 1 when the low six are usable), and a unit that has SHAPES at start 0 gets it moved by the page (an install beside: the planner already names the other start and erases the old copy after the new one reads back); (b) the models module takes the lowest start of the longest run; (c) the page's order becomes SHAPES, the models, then the small modules. Not built: this is the next piece of option A, with its own review, and it changes where modules land on every unit.

**Round 23 (the three sector tail): the goal set is one sector short.** The set of the paragraph above is 22 sectors (SHAPES 6, the pick 9, WHEEL 1, the FX layer 2, DUB TAPE 3, a reverb mode 1) and the unit now has 21 (18 + 3). What fits: SHAPES at start 1, the models at -6..2, WHEEL at sector 3 (16 of the area's 18), DUB TAPE in the tail, which it fills exactly (its only tail start is -58), and **one** of the two small modules in the area's last two sectors (4, 5): the 2 sector FX layer (21 of 21) or a 1 sector reverb mode (20 of 21), not both. **Built under the switch (2026-11-10, after the finding above; `OC_MOD_TAIL=3` builds only (4 as first built), a default build is unchanged):**

- **The placement rule** (`oc_module.c` `oc_plan_score`, used by `oc_mod_plan` and `oc_fxmod_plan`; `web/fm1place.js` `score` / `besideBest`). Of the starts where the new module stands beside everything (an empty start first, then one that holds junk), the planner takes the one that leaves **the longest free run on the unit**, then **the fewest free runs**, then **the lowest sector** (the tail's are the lowest, then the area upward from 0xC2000). In place (no start beside) is as before. A sector is free when no usable module's reach covers it and it may be used (the low six only once they are the area's); a run lies in one place. The effect of the three keys: SHAPES goes to start 1 on an empty unit whose low six are usable (twelve left in one run; start 0 would leave six and six) and to start 0 when they are not (a tie: the lowest sector, as every unit today); a pick goes to the bottom of the area; a small module goes to the tail unless that would shorten the longest run.

- **Rearrange** (`fm1place.js` `arrange`; `fm1oc.js` `arrangementOf`, `rearrangeCheck`, `removeModule`, `runSet`). The planner is asked three things in turn, on the unit as it was just read. (1) Does the set, less the modules the unit already holds as these files, fit as the unit stands, in the goal's order? Then it is installed so, nothing asked. (2) If not: does the **whole** set fit on the same unit with **the set's own modules** taken off? Then the page offers the rearrange. Before it asks it checks every file of the set against the unit and the start the plan gives it (the firmware's numbers, an image for that start, its inner header and CRC, the sector tails, an effect module's calling convention), so nothing is taken off that could not be put back; a file that fails is reported and nothing is asked or removed. On no (or a caller with no question to ask) nothing is removed or written. (3) If not: would it fit without modules that are **not** of the set? Those are **never removed**. The page names them with its existing text, one sentence a module: "ENGINE {name} INSTALLED AT START {n}: REMOVE IT FIRST" (an effect module by its name, DUB TAPE, or "EFFECT 0x{id}"). - **A failure in the middle.** A rearrange stops at its first failure and writes nothing more. The first removal refused (the transport is running): nothing was removed, the unit's own reason is shown ("Stop playback first"). A later removal or an install refused: the summary shows what is back ("installed"), the failure's own text, and "Not put back yet: press Install again." for the rest, with the line "The rearrangement stopped part way. ### 10.6 The updates, power cuts and downgrades

- A cut in any window of an update: the windows are the loader's as before (before the first write; marker erased; stub sector; each body sector; marker written); the tail is outside all of them, so **no window can damage a module**. A cut leaves the stub's rule (no marker: rescue). Host proof: the NOR simulator's power-cut test (`tests/loader_powercut_test.c`) cuts at every program and erase of an update over a unit image with modules in the tail and checks the tail bytes are identical after every cut and after the completed update, and that the body is old, new or refused as before. ### 10.9 The independent review

Independent review (subagent, read only, 2026-11-10). Verdict: **the design is not buildable as written; two blockers, seven should-fix.** The design above is amended by this list (it wins where they differ).

**Blocking**
1. **The stub reads the commit marker through XIP** (stub.c:295: XIP 0x91FE0, expects hardware-decrypted bytes); the plain window is enabled in the stub only on its rescue/test paths (stub.c:260, stub_core.h:262). Moving `SFCENC_UNENC_L` to 0x8D000 puts 0x91000 (the marker) inside the plaintext window. If the window register survives a warm reset (watchdog, `fm1_core_reset`, the stub's REQ_TEST restart), the marker reads as ciphertext and the stub rescues at every restart after the app has run. Today's 0x93000 bound never touched the marker. Nothing in the repo says whether the register resets; host tests cannot show it. **10.4's "nothing reads them that way" is wrong.** First bench gate, before any other A work ships: three cases (WDT reset, core reset, REQ_TEST restart) with the window at 0x8D000. If the register persists, the app must clear SFCENC_CON bit 1 on every orderly reset path, a WDT or fault reset cannot be covered, and the stub would have to change (which this design rules out). **Until this is measured on the unit, the window stays at 0x93000, and the tail region must not be enabled in any firmware.** (Alternative to evaluate on the bench: keep the window at 0x93000 and read tail modules through the encrypted path by writing them encrypted: costs the installer an encrypt step and the module code a decrypting read; not designed.)
2. **Span and erase code breaks for tail starts**: `OC_MOD_START_SECTOR` is uint32 (oc_module.c:119) and wraps for rel -59; `oc_mod_erase_span` (879) loops on `first+s < OC_MOD_AREA`, so for a tail start it erases nothing and returns 0 (remove "succeeds", begin programs over unerased flash); same assumption at 125, 152, 162, 178-179 (the models id's 18 max), 383, 502, 861-865, 894, 907-908, 1075, 1103, 1320; `fm1place.js` has the same with negative numbers (plans a 9..18 sector module into the 4-sector tail). **Change: absolute flash sectors with per-place [lo, hi) bounds and a per-place maximum span** (4 in the tail; the models cap = min(18, place)); `oc_mod_erase_span` refuses any span crossing a place boundary (unit test); `FL_STORE_OK` gains exactly 0x8D000..0x91000 and a `_Static_assert` like storage.c:88.
