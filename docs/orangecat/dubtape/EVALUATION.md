# DUB-TAPE: evaluation

All numbers come from `make all-results`. The full tables are in `MEASUREMENTS.md` (generated by `tools/measure.c`),
`BENCH_HOST.md` (`tools/bench.c`) and `RENDERS.csv` (`tools/render.c`). The FM-1 instruction counts come from `make fm1`.
Configurations: **DIGITAL** = conventional reference (one head, flat feedback, hard clamp, no filters); **LITE** =
algorithm A; **TAPE** = algorithm B; 44k/22k/11k = loop rate 44.1 / 22.05 / 11.025 kHz (host always 44.1 kHz, 128-frame
blocks).

**What has not been done:** no listening test by a person, no comparison against any hardware unit or reference
recording, no execution on the FM-1. Nothing here claims to match an RE-201 or any other unit. The FM-1 CPU figures are
static instruction counts and host timings, i.e. estimates and proxies.

## 1. Renders (listening comparisons)

`renders/<scene>_<variant>.wav`: 16-bit stereo, 44.1 kHz, TPDF-dithered. Variants: `0-dry`, `1-digital`, `2/3/4-lite
44k/22k/11k`, `5/6/7-tape 44k/22k/11k`, `8-tape22k-stereo` (two lines, cross-feedback; skank/throw/atmos),
`9-lite22k-cheapAA` (box/linear converters; skank/throw). The 44.1 kHz variants use lines larger than the FM-1 budget
(64 KB for 0.74 s; `RENDERS.csv` lists each render's line size). They are included for comparison.

| Scene | Source (known content) | What it demonstrates |
|---|---|---|
| `skank` (11 s, 75 BPM) | Cm7 stab (C4 Eb4 G4 Bb4, band-limited saws, 2 ms attack, 160 ms gate) on beats 2 and 4, two bars | SKANK heads (1/8 + dotted 1/8, feedback from the dotted head). FEEDBACK ridden 0.50 → 0.78 at bar 2; LP closed 3.0 → 0.9 kHz over the tail |
| `slap` (8 s) | synthetic snare (160→185 Hz body + 330 Hz partial, 4 kHz band-passed noise, click) on beat 3 of bars 1 and 2 | SLAP heads (1/16 + dotted 1/8), FEEDBACK 0.35. Hit 1 thin/bright (HP 583 Hz, LP 5.7 kHz, RES 0.55); hit 2 dark/full (HP 92 Hz, LP 1.55 kHz) |
| `throw` (12 s) | 250 ms vowel "ah" (140 Hz pulse, formants 700/1220/2600 Hz) | SEND open 0.25–0.6 s only; TRIPLE heads 200/400/600 ms; FEEDBACK 0.88, RES 0.65; the LP swept 3.5 kHz → 470 Hz → 7.1 kHz → 1.55 kHz while the loop regenerates by itself |
| `oscillate` (18 s) | one 5 ms noise click | FEEDBACK 0.95 → 1.2 (held 3–13 s) → 0.5; LP wobbling 2.0–4.6 kHz; DRIVE 0.6. Bounded growth, then recovery |
| `atmos` (12 s) | a stab, then a vowel | long atmospheric: TRIPLE at 400 ms (heads to 1.2 s: 32 KB only at 11.025 kHz), WOW 0.6, dark LP |

Envelope check (`tools/envelope.py`, RMS per 0.5 s): with the `throw`, DIGITAL holds −24…−40 dBFS for 11 s (no
filtering, flat feedback); LITE/TAPE decay from −17 to −80 dBFS over 11 s while the swept filter colours them. In
`oscillate`, all LITE/TAPE variants grow steadily (about +2 dB/s) while FEEDBACK > 1 and fall back once it drops.

## 2. Echo timing (MEASUREMENTS §1)

Impulse into SKANK heads (2D, 3D, D = 150 ms) with feedback from 3D; expected echoes at 2,3,5,6,8,9 D.

| | first echoes | per pass | cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| LITE 44k | exact (0.00 samples) | +0.2…0.3 sample | group delay of the loop filters (inherent to any filtered loop; ≈ 5 µs) |
| TAPE 44k | +0.49 | +0.5…0.7 | as above plus the ADAA saturator's ½ sample |
| 22k / 11k | +0…4 (44.1 kHz samples) | ≤ +0.5 loop sample (≤ 23 / 45 µs) | heads on whole loop samples; constant converter latency 29 / 87 samples (0.66 / 1.97 ms) reported separately |

Worst case after 20 repeats at 11.025 kHz: < 1 ms. Musically negligible, and tempo-synced patterns stay locked. With
WOW = FLUTTER = 0.5 the echoes deviate by up to ±1.7 ms, coherently across heads (intended).

## 3. Echo decay and spectral evolution (MEASUREMENTS §1, §3)

* Echo energy ratio per repeat at FEEDBACK 0.5 with filters open: −6.4…−7.5 dB (ideal −6.02). The extra 0.4–1.4 dB is the
  open filters' residual loss (HP 20 Hz, LP clamped at 0.45 f_l, Q 0.5) plus ADAA averaging in TAPE.
* Noise burst, one head, FEEDBACK 0.85, HP 196 Hz, LP 3.0 kHz, RES 0.3, relative to the first repeat:

| repeat 8 vs 1, octave bands | 63 | 125 | 250 | 500 | 1k | 2k | 4k | 8k |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DIGITAL | −9.9 | −9.9 | −9.9 | −9.9 | −9.9 | −9.9 | −9.9 | −9.9 |
| LITE 44k | −66 | −40 | −22 | −14 | −12 | −18 | −43 | −84 |
| LITE 22k | −64 | −40 | −22 | −14 | −12 | −18 | −43 | −80 |
| TAPE 44k | −71 | −39 | −10 | −8 | −11 | −18 | −44 | −81 |
| TAPE 22k | −70 | −39 | −10 | −8 | −11 | −19 | −48 | −73 |

  The conventional delay keeps its spectrum; DUB-TAPE narrows each pass toward a 0.5–1 kHz band, which is the
  "progressive filtering". The 22 kHz rows match the 44 kHz rows within ~1 dB below 4 kHz. TAPE's resonant HP keeps more
  low-mid (250–500 Hz) body. At 11 kHz the 8 kHz band is the line's noise/Nyquist floor, not signal.

## 4. Feedback stability (MEASUREMENTS §4)

60 s per case, FEEDBACK 1.2, every corner of RES (0/1), LP (0/60/90/127) and DRIVE (0/1), seeded with −60 dBFS noise:
all outputs finite, peak ≤ −0.2 dBFS (the output limiter's ceiling is 0 dBFS), DC ≤ 4e-4. Runaways settle where the saturator holds the loop gain at 1:
−2.5 dBFS RMS at DRIVE 0 / LP open (the loudest case), −15 dBFS at DRIVE 1. With the LP at its lowest knob or RES 1 at
20 kHz the loop dies out instead: the resonance sits where the line/ADAA no longer passes. A 60 s random-parameter fuzz
(every parameter, new values every 20 ms, FEEDBACK to 1.2, TIME 20–320 ms) stays finite with peaks ≤ −0.18 dBFS for every
configuration.

## 5. Saturation and aliasing (MEASUREMENTS §5)

One pass, 1 kHz sine: odd harmonics only. THD at DRIVE 0 (LITE / TAPE): −68 / −69 dB (−20 dBFS), −40 / −41 dB (−6 dBFS),
−28 / −29 dB (0 dBFS). At DRIVE 1 from −6 dBFS: −14 / −15 dB (heavy). Inharmonic residue (noise + aliasing) at 1 kHz
stays at or below −79 dB.

Aliasing, 4 kHz sine (alias + noise re fundamental, dB):

| | DRIVE 0.5, −6 dBFS | DRIVE 1, −6 dBFS | DRIVE 1, 0 dBFS |
|---|---|---|---|
| LITE 44k | −96 | −43 | −23 |
| TAPE 44k (ADAA) | −95 | −56 | −34 |
| LITE 22k | −32 | −16 | −11 |
| TAPE 22k | −32 | −22 | −21 |
| LITE 22k, record route (LP 4.6 kHz) | −42 | −26 | −14 |
| TAPE 22k, record route | −43 | −26 | −22 |

Reading: at 44.1 kHz the plain cubic is clean until it is driven hard, and ADAA buys 12–13 dB there. At 22.05 kHz,
saturating a strong 4 kHz component aliases audibly (the 3rd harmonic folds to 10 kHz). Record routing helps ~10 dB;
ADAA does not help. For dub material (loop LP usually 1–3 kHz, sources rarely loud above 4 kHz) this is a modest
"grit". For bright, driven presets it is the main sonic cost of the reduced rate.

## 6. Noise (MEASUREMENTS §6)

int16 line, −20 dBFS 1 kHz tone: noise + distortion residue −77…−80 dB at FEEDBACK 0; −51…−65 dB at FEEDBACK 0.9 (the
residue includes the loop's saturator products and regenerated quantisation). Idle output after a tail: −78…−95 dBFS
(TAPE higher: ADAA plus emphasis keep a low-level limit cycle above the int16 dead zone). An option is TPDF dither on the
write, ~1 LCG and 2 adds a sample, not prototyped. µ-law/8-bit storage would put this around −38 dB and is not recommended
for a regenerating loop. 12-bit packing (~−72 dB) is plausible, with packing cost.

## 7. Parameter-change artifacts (MEASUREMENTS §7, §8)

Splatter above 5 kHz around the event, 220 Hz sine at −12 dBFS (a hard step reads around −22 dBFS):

| event | result |
|---|---|
| TIME 200 → 247 ms, GLIDE 0 (jump) | −22 dBFS at 44.1/22.05 kHz (a click) |
| TIME 200 → 247 ms, GLIDE 0 (jump) at 11.025 kHz | −35 dBFS |
| TIME, GLIDE 0.12 s (tape-style) | −69…−77 dBFS. The cost is a pitch swoop: computed peak ≈ 870 cents down for this 24 % change (the one-pole's initial slope); a longer GLIDE or smaller change bends less |
| LP sweep 60 → 110 in 1 s | −96…−99 dBFS (no zipper at 32-sample coefficient updates) |
| pattern change SINGLE → TRIPLE | −86…−104 dBFS |
| FEEDBACK 0 → 0.9 | −77…−95 dBFS |
| WET 1 → 0 (bypass) | −73…−80 dBFS |
| SEND 1 → 0 (bypass with tails) | −96…−101 dBFS |
| WET 0, DRY 1 | output **bit-exact** to the input (max difference 0), zero latency |

## 8. Resources

### RAM

int16 line: **88,200 B per second at 44.1 kHz, 44,100 B/s at 22.05 kHz, 22,050 B/s at 11.025 kHz** (×2 for float, ×2
per extra stereo line). With power-of-two lines:

| line | 44.1 kHz | 22.05 kHz | 11.025 kHz |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 KB (4096) | 0.093 s | 0.186 s | 0.371 s |
| 16 KB (8192) | 0.186 s | 0.371 s | 0.743 s |
| **32 KB (16384)** | 0.371 s | **0.743 s** | **1.486 s** |
| 64 KB (32768) | 0.743 s | 1.486 s | 2.972 s |

An exact-length line with a compare-and-wrap instead of a mask saves the power-of-two rounding (0.6 s at 22.05 kHz = 26 KB
instead of 32 KB). Cost: one compare per read.

State (this prototype's `dd_t`, all options): 2,672 B, of which 1,536 B are per-rate coefficient tables that can be
shared between instances, and 816 B are converter histories (not needed at R = 1). LITE-only mutable state ≈ 0.3–1.1 KB.
Stack in `dd_process` ≈ 3.2 KB (block buffers; move to static scratch on the FM-1).

| configuration | 1 instance | 4 instances |
|---|---|---|
| LITE 22k, 0.74 s (32 KB) | 32 KB + ~1.1 KB | 132 KB: over the 48 KiB arena |
| LITE 11k, 1.49 s (32 KB) | 32 KB + ~1.1 KB | 132 KB: over |
| LITE 22k, 0.37 s slap (16 KB) | 16 KB + ~1.1 KB | 68 KB: over |
| LITE 11k, 0.37 s slap (8 KB) | 8 KB + ~1.1 KB | 36 KB: fits, short slaps only |
| TAPE 22k stereo, 0.74 s (2 × 32 KB) | 64 KB: over | n/a |
| TAPE 22k mono, 0.74 s | 32 KB + ~2.7 KB | 140 KB: over |

### CPU

FM-1 static instruction counts (pi32v2 clang -O2, Orange Cat's flags; `make fm1`; instructions, **not cycles**; the
estimate assumes ~1 instruction/cycle at 360 MHz with no stall model):

| block | instructions |
|---|---|
| LITE loop | 129 a loop sample + 52 per active head (≈ 6 per idle slot) |
| TAPE loop, 1 line | 56 + 124 per active head + 176 per line |
| half-band decimator | 102 an output (≈ 10 otherwise) |
| half-band interpolator | 94 an input (2 outputs), per channel |
| control tick (32 host samples) | ≈ 400 (LITE) … 1,000 (TAPE) |

| configuration | est. instr/s | ≈ % of one 360 MHz core | host proxy, µs/block (median, 1 / 4 inst.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| DIGITAL 44k, 1 head | ~6 M (not counted separately; LITE minus filters/saturator) | ~1.7 % | 8.5 / 26 |
| LITE 44k, 2 heads | ~11 M | ~3 % | 10.9 / 38 |
| LITE 22k, 2 heads | ~5.5 M loop + ~6.6 M conv ≈ 12 M | ~3.4 % | 11.8 / 41 |
| LITE 11k, 2 heads | ~2.7 M + ~10 M ≈ 13 M | ~3.6 % | 12.2 / 42 |
| TAPE 44k, 3 heads | ~28 M | ~7.7 % | 23.2 / 87 |
| TAPE 22k, 3 heads | ~14 M + ~6.6 M ≈ 21 M | ~5.8 % | 18.1 / 67 |
| TAPE 11k, 3 heads | ~7 M + ~10 M ≈ 18 M | ~5 % | 15.6 / 57 |
| TAPE 22k stereo, 3 heads | ~22 M + ~6.6 M ≈ 30 M | ~8.3 % | 24.8 / 93 |

Four instances scale linearly (×4). Host proxy: Intel i5-6260U, per-thread CPU time, median over 30 s (the machine was
under a load average of ~18 during the run, so the p99 columns in BENCH_HOST.md carry noise). The 2,901 µs block deadline
is not comparable with x86 timings.

**Interpolation and modulation overhead.** Hermite costs ~+25 instructions a head over linear. Tape-consistent modulation
costs ~140 instructions a head **a tick**, i.e. ~4 a head a sample. Computing it per sample would cost ~35×
more for no audible difference at 0.55/6.3 Hz.

**Reduced rate saves RAM, not CPU.** At R = 2 the loop cost halves, but the converters (≈ 150 instructions a host sample
for mono-in / stereo-out) cost about as much as a whole LITE loop at 44.1 kHz. Two cheaper paths, not yet measured: a
shorter output interpolator (15 taps), justified because the loop LP usually removes content above ~5 kHz; or
interpolating a mono wet plus one panned "side" head. For TAPE (heavier loop) the reduced rate does save CPU.

## 9. Comparison

| | DIGITAL (reference) | A: DUB-LITE | B: DUB-TAPE |
|---|---|---|---|
| sonic character | static repeats, no colour | dub chain: progressive band narrowing, resonant regeneration, bounded runaway, tape-style time glide | as A, plus coherent wow/flutter, HF-first saturation, more low-mid body, ADAA (cleaner drive at 44.1 kHz), optional true ping-pong |
| aliasing when driven (44.1 kHz) | none (no saturator) | −43 dB at DRIVE 1/−6 dBFS | −56 dB |
| CPU (22.05 kHz, FM-1 est.) | — | ~3.4 % | ~5.8 % |
| RAM (0.74 s) | — | 32 KB + ~1 KB | 32 KB + ~2.7 KB (×2 stereo) |
| code size (pi32v2 -O2) | — | run_lite 243 instructions | run_tape 429 instructions; whole library ≈ 2.4 k instructions |
| portability | trivial | C99, no libm, fixed iteration counts | same |
